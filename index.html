Our approach

Your soil supplies most of it. We manage the gap.

Your soil already supplies a large share of what the crop needs. Our job is to measure that supply, match it to what this year's crop actually needs, and fill only the difference.

That's a different starting point than a blanket pounds-per-bushel rate set in winter. With Agronomy 365 we read the full fertility bank, set yield goals by zone from biomass history, and adjust in season as soil and tissue samples come back.

Trial example

Soybean seeding rate by zone

Field-scale strips · 5 rates × 2 zones · harvested, weighed and grain sampled
Seeds/ac100k125k150k175k200k
Zone 3 Productive, deep soil
Yield, bu/ac48.150.351.251.651.7
Net, $/ac$438$449$448$441$431
Zone 1 Eroded hilltop
Yield, bu/ac36.039.441.843.043.4
Net, $/ac$317$340$354$355$348

What it told us: the most bushels came at 200k in both zones, but the most profit didn't. The productive zone paid best at 125k and the hilltop at 175k. That became the seed script: about $8 more per acre on the good ground than a flat 175k, without shorting the tough acres.

Efficiency drives profitability

2,500 acres of irrigated and dryland corn, strip-till with Y-drop and 2x2, on Baseline Rx since 2019. Same zones, same sampling, six seasons of connected data.

What drives efficiency?WEOC. More water-extractable carbon feeds soil biology and builds structure, so the soil cycles and releases more nutrients to the crop.
How do we know?Bushels per pound of applied nutrient went up every year. The crop didn't need less. The soil supplied more of it.
